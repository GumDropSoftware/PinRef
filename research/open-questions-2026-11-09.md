# PinRef open questions — research for Jadon
2026-11-09. Written for a phone: short paragraphs, no tables. Every claim names its source. Sources I opened myself in Chrome are cited plainly; the handful of facts that came only from the five research subagents (they fetch pages, they do not use Chrome) are marked "agent-reported" and should be treated as leads, not confirmed facts. Where a manual and a support page disagree, the manual wins and the disagreement is called out.

How each question is laid out: the gear involved; every convention found and who is behind it; what engineers actually build and buy; the risks (power, phantom, damage, crosstalk, polarity, breaking cables already built); what each of the five review angles (documentation, power and damage, circuit, field practice, red team) concluded; a recommendation with a confidence level; and what, if anything, changed in PinRef because of it.

## Q1. Is "stereo on an XLR3, pin 2 left, pin 3 right, pin 1 ground" a real thing?

Where it would show up. IFB earpiece feeds to talent, beltpack headphone outputs, stereo program to a 5-pin headset, wireless in-ear monitor packs. The XLR3 is everywhere in a truck, so people are tempted to use it for anything with two signals.

What the manufacturers actually do. Clear-Com puts stereo on the 5-pin headset connector, never on a 3-pin: XLR-5F pin 3 is Headphone Return, pin 4 Left, pin 5 Right (Clear-Com V-Series online manual, "Headset connectors"). Its 3-pin intercom line is pin 1 Ground, pin 2 Voltage, pin 3 Audio (Clear-Com Solution Finder, "What is the 3-pin XLR line pin-out?"). Its 6-pin IFB and split-ear connectors put +30 V DC on pin 2 (Clear-Com Solution Finder, "6-pin XLR wiring").
RTS does the same. The BP-325 manual's Specifications page (manualslib, page 13) puts stereo on the XLR-5-31: pin 3 Common, pin 4 Headphone left high, pin 5 Headphone right high. Its 3-pin line input (XLR-3-31) is pin 1 Common, pin 2 "Channel 1 intercom audio and +DC", pin 3 Channel 2 intercom audio. RTS stereo IFB earsets live on a TRS, not an XLR3: Tip is the interrupt feed, Ring is non-interrupt program, Sleeve common (RTS IFB-828 User Manual, page 6).
Telex's party-line design manual describes the closest thing to a stereo XLR3, and it is the opposite of left/right: in "pseudo-stereo mode" you tie the high side of ONE pair to pins 2 and 3 and the low side to pin 1, so the talent gets one mono feed in both ears with each ear adjustable (Telex manual 38109-977, chapter 3, page 33). That is mono-to-both, not stereo.
Wikipedia's XLR article documents XLR3 for balanced audio and XLR4 for balanced headphones; there is no stereo-on-XLR3 standard in it. Agent-reported: the Shure PSM300 guide gives the bodypack output as a 3.5 mm stereo jack, and David Clark's stereo headsets use the 5-pin layout (red to 2, yellow to 4, green to 5, returns to 3); the only "3-pin stereo" that exists is AKG's mini-XLR on studio headphones, a different connector.

The power problem. On every intercom 3-pin connector in broadcast, pin 2 carries DC: 30 V on Clear-Com and RTS party line and IFB, 15 to 30 V on the Clear-Com RS-701 intercom connector (RS-701 Quick Reference, "Intercom connector pins"). A "stereo" XLR3 with a headphone driver from pin 2 to pin 1, plugged into a party-line jack by mistake, puts 30 V across a 200 to 400 ohm earpiece: roughly 75 to 150 mA through a coil built for milliwatts, a pop in the talent's ear, a cooked driver, and because that coil is now a short across the power rail the station supply folds back and every pack on the channel dies until the adapter is pulled. On a mixer input pins 2 and 3 carry 48 V phantom through 6.8 k resistors: both drivers sit on DC and the diaphragms are offset.

The circuit problem. If the "stereo" XLR3 meets a balanced input, the input amplifies the difference between pins 2 and 3: left minus right, so everything in the centre cancels (Rane Note 151 on what a balanced input rejects). Driven from a balanced output, the "right" ear gets an inverted copy of the left: a hollow, no-centre image that sounds like stereo and gets shipped. Both ear currents also return through the single pin-1 conductor, so its resistance appears in series with both channels and one channel's return shows up as the other's signal (common-impedance coupling); Rane caps unbalanced runs at about ten feet for that reason (Rane Note 110). If two beltpack earphone outputs are bridged to make such a cable "mono", two amplifier outputs are shorted together: Clear-Com says that distorts the whole party-line channel (Solution Finder, 4-pin to 5-pin adaptor) and Rane Note 109 says never tie two outputs together.

The five angles. Documentation: no manual or standard defines it; where stereo exists it is on five pins or a jack. Power: pin 2 is a DC pin on every intercom XLR3, so this cable is a live-voltage hazard the moment it is mis-plugged, and the pin-3 load drags the party-line audio bus down for everyone. Circuit: fails into any balanced input, imports the TRS stereo-versus-balanced ambiguity onto a connector that has no such ambiguity today. Field: nobody sells it; rental shops use TRS or XLR5 for stereo. Red team: identical shell to a mic cable, impossible to tell apart on a bench, and the Telex pseudo-stereo wording will make a tech who believes "2 = L, 3 = R" report "program is in mono" and start swapping good cables.

Recommendation. Do not bring it back. If a stereo feed must ride a 3-pin shell, build it only as a labelled pair with the matching breakout (the way the XLR3-to-XLR5 Pass-Through already works) and never let it near an intercom or IFB jack. Confidence: high that no convention exists; high on the risk.
What changed in PinRef: nothing new this pass; the stereo-on-XLR3 wiring was removed in Pass 2 and the Pass-Through build now says why.
Decision (Jadon, 2026-11-09): leave it out. PinRef has no stereo-on-XLR3 build and will not add one; stereo stays on XLR5 or a jack.

## Q2. XLR4 and XLR5 headset signals over Cat5. How should the pins be paired?

Where it shows up. Running a headset or a beltpack to a far position down house Cat5, or using Cat5 as a long headset extension in a truck compound.

What the makers actually publish. Nobody publishes a headset-over-RJ45 pinout; the four Cat5 audio sheets I opened all carry balanced line pairs or a party line, which is a different job. Clear-Com 4-wire uses the blue pair (4/5) and the green pair (3/6) for the two balanced audio directions, each pair to XLR pins 2 and 3 (Clear-Com Solution Finder, 4-wire RJ45 adapters). Clear-Com HelixNet runs its powered party line over Cat5 with the orange pair (1/2) as XLR pin 1 and the other six conductors as the +30 V and −30 V legs; the HelixNet tip page on clearcom.com puts +30 V on RJ45 3, 6 and 7 and −30 V on 4, 5 and 8, while the Solution Finder HelixNet page puts pin 2 on 4, 6, 8 and pin 3 on 3, 5, 7. The two Clear-Com documents disagree on which pins carry which polarity; they agree that 1/2 is ground and 3 to 8 carry roughly 30 V. Either way a headset RJ45 in a HelixNet port is destroyed. StudioHub+ (Angry Audio) puts left on 1/2, right on 3/6, DC ground on 4, 15 V rails on 7 and 8 (StudioHub+ Adapter Pinout Guide). Whirlwind Catdusa and Radial Catapult put one balanced channel per pair, in different orders (Catdusa manual page 2; Radial Catapult specifications). Every one of them keeps a signal and its return in the SAME twisted pair.

What Clear-Com says about extending a headset at all. Three Solution Finder pages carry the same text: extensions limited to 15 ft because capacitive coupling between the mic signal and the headset signal causes loss of high frequencies or oscillation; "DO NOT connect microphone ground and earphone ground together at any point"; "Pins one and two should be on a separate shielded pair from pins 3 and 4"; use a two-pair individually shielded snake cable such as Canare MR202-2AT (Clear-Com Solution Finder pages 158845384, 158755357, 158748532). That is the manufacturer rule for the circuit, and it answers the pairing question directly.

What the headset circuit is. The intercom mic input is unbalanced: Clear-Com states that on the RTS pack "mic signal low" is actually signal ground, and its own 4-pin pinout names pin 1 Mic Ground and pin 3 Headset Ground (Clear-Com Solution Finder pages 158841161 and 158685542). So there are two circuits, mic (pins 1 and 2) and earphone (pin 3 return with pins 4 and 5 hot), and the manufacturers' own labels put 1 with 2 and 3 with 4/5. Clear-Com's wiring of a Beyer DT-108 does exactly that: pin 1 mic shield, pin 2 mic+, pin 3 the two ear minus leads, pin 4 the two ear plus leads (agent-reported, Solution Finder 158851068). The level gap is enormous: a dynamic headset mic is about −55 dBu, while the BP-325 headphone amplifier swings 8 V peak-to-peak into 50 to 600 ohms (BP-325 manual page 13). That is roughly 70 dB; a pair that twists the mic hot around the earphone return turns the earphone programme into the mic's loop partner, and because every beltpack returns your own mic to your ear as sidetone, that coupling closes a loop and becomes a howl, not just a hum.

What PinRef had, and what it has now. The earlier PinRef builds twisted mic signal (RJ45 1) with the EARPHONE return (RJ45 2) and put mic ground alone on pin 4: the worst pairing by the theory above and the opposite of Clear-Com's rule. This pass re-pinned all four builds (XLR5 to RJ45 and back, XLR4 to RJ45 and back): mic signal and mic ground on the orange pair (1/2); left ear and ear return on the green pair (3/6); for the 5-pin builds the right ear on the blue pair with a second conductor bonded to pin 3 as its return (5/4); brown pair unused; shield drain, if any, to the XLR shell at the beltpack end only, never to pin 1 and pin 3 together. The old pairing is described in each build's notes so cables already built can be identified.

Power on the line. The pack's electret bias (2.5 V on pin 2 versus pin 1 on FreeSpeak II, per the FSII-BP24 specification, agent-reported) rides down the mic pair. A Clear-Com RS-700 pack puts about 15 V DC between headset pin 4 and pin 1 (RS-701 Quick Reference), which lands on two different pairs of any headset-over-Cat5 cable. An RJ45 on the end of a headset cable also fits an Ethernet switch, a PoE port (44 to 57 V on pairs 1/2 and 3/6 in Mode A, or 4/5 and 7/8 in Mode B; Wikipedia, Power over Ethernet), a powered StudioHub+ panel (15 V on 7/8) and a HelixNet powerline port (30 V on pins 3 to 8). The earlier PinRef notes named only the Mode-B PoE pins, which are exactly the pins the headset builds leave open; the live pins are the Mode-A ones. Fixed.

The five angles. Documentation: no manufacturer pinout exists; the sources that do exist (Clear-Com extension rule, headset pinouts, Beyer wiring) all keep 1 with 2 and 3 with 4. Power: PoE Mode A, HelixNet and the RS-700 15 V are the real hazards; the old notes named the wrong PoE pins and did not mention HelixNet or the 15 V. Circuit: the old pairing was wrong by twisted-pair theory; the new one is the only arrangement every cited document supports. Field: engineers extend headsets on XLR, not Cat5, and when they use Cat5 they use a manufacturer box; whatever a shop draws, both ends must match and the shells must be labelled. Red team: the worst case is the RJ45 going into an intercom powerline or PoE injector (30 to 50 V across the mic capsule and earphones), then two pairing conventions mixed on one run (headphone amp into the mic preamp).

Recommendation. Keep the new pairing. Re-terminate any existing headset-over-Cat5 cables at BOTH ends or keep them as a labelled matched pair, and never mix old and new on one run. Mark both RJ45 plugs HEADSET ONLY. Keep runs short (Clear-Com says 15 ft for its own headset extensions) and use shielded Cat5 with the drain on the XLR shell at the pack end. Confidence: high on the theory and the manufacturer rule; the decision to re-pin cables already in the kit is Jadon's.
What changed in PinRef: builds 18, 34, 36 and 51 re-pinned as above, genders stated per end, PoE and HelixNet cautions corrected, cross-reference numbers fixed.
Decision (Jadon, 2026-11-09): keep the new separate-pair layout. He has no cables built to the old one, so the "re-terminate existing cables" sentence was removed from builds 18, 34, 36 and 51.

## Q3. StudioHub+ ground pinning.

Where it shows up. Radio and TV studios wired on StudioHub+ or Axia/Telos nodes, where every analog and AES circuit lands on an RJ45 and adapters hang off the back of the gear.

What the sheet says. The StudioHub+ Adapter Pinout Guide routes XLR pin 2 to RJ45 pin 1, pin 3 to pin 2 and XLR pin 1 to "Shield", meaning the cable shield and shielded plug, not a numbered pin; the universal pinout lists pin 4 as DC GND, pin 5 as n/c, 7 as 15 V minus and 8 as 15 V plus. Telos says Axia's RJ45 audio is "commonly referred to as the StudioHub wiring standard" and, on phantom power: you must use shielded Cat-5 because the ground for phantom is carried on the shield; unshielded cable means no ground, no phantom, no working condenser mic (Telos docs, "Microphone phantom power when using RJ45 adapters"). Whirlwind says the same for the Catdusa: shielded Cat5 or Cat6 must be used to connect pin 1 box to box and to pass phantom (Catdusa manual page 2).

What StudioHub's own hardware does. The SH-RACK4 panel manual shows that "pin 1 to pin 4" is one of StudioHub's two factory jumper positions. On the SH-2XLRF dual XLR panel the "RJ45 shield and ground select jumpers" are labelled "Install to connect XLR Pin 1 to RJ-45 shield" and "Install to connect XLR Pin 1 to RJ-45 power ground (RJ-45 pin 4)", with a third jumper for pin 1 to chassis (SH-RACK4 manual, page 13); the single-XLR panels have the same JU1/JU2 choice (pages 16 and 17). So landing XLR pin 1 on RJ45 pin 4 is a StudioHub-sanctioned option, not a PinRef invention. Pin 5 is different: the sheet keeps it n/c, and agent-reported from the same manual, the SH-1XLRM mic panel uses pin 5 to light a mic-boom LED when grounded. A cable that grounds pin 5 would leave that lamp stuck on.

The unbalanced adapters settle a second point. The ADAPT-MINIUNBAL row of the guide, read at 90% zoom, brackets the 3.5 mm sleeve to Pin-2, Pin-6 AND the shield together: the official stereo-mini adapter returns Left on pin 2 and Right on pin 6 (each channel's own pair-mate), not on pin 4. The "n/c" marks on that row mean the plug has no separate contact for those pins, not that they are open. The ADAPT-RCA row does the same for mono (positive to pin 1, shield to pin 2 and shield). PinRef's 3.5 mm-to-RJ45 build had the sleeve on pin 4 with pins 2 and 6 open; against a real StudioHub+ panel that left both cold inputs floating and, on a hub channel with DC-link jumpers in, rode the audio ground on the ±15 V return. It now returns on 2 and 6 as the guide shows.

What breaks under each choice. Shield-only on unshielded Cat5: XLR pin 1 goes nowhere. Balanced line level still passes, so the cable "works" on the bench and goes into service; then a condenser mic or an unbalanced device goes through it and is dead or hums, and nobody suspects the cable (Telos and Whirlwind both describe this). Pin 1 on pin 4: pin 1 works over UTP and phantom has a return, but pin 1 and the StudioHub DC ground are now one net. On a passive adapter nothing happens; on a run that also carries the ±15 V rails to a powered StudioHub box, the supply's return current and ripple flow in the audio ground conductor, and between two mains-earthed devices it is the classic ground loop (Rane Note 151). Pin 1 on pin 5 as well: pin 5 is n/c on StudioHub+, is a channel hot or cold on Radial, Whirlwind and RAT/ETS snake boxes (grounding a live leg), and is the boom-LED line on the StudioHub mic panel. There is no reason to ground it. The RJ45 also fits a PoE port: Mode B puts 48 V on 4/5 against 7/8, so a pin-4 ground cable plugged into a switch puts 48 V onto XLR pin 1 and the gear chassis.

The five angles. Documentation: the sheet says shield; the SH-RACK4 manual shows pin 4 as a factory alternative; nothing anywhere grounds pin 5. Power: pin 4 is safe on a StudioHub+ panel and makes pin 1 the supply return on a powered run; the dangerous mis-plug is PoE, and the other RJ45 snake boxes with a signal pair on 4/5. Circuit: pins 4 and 5 are one twisted pair, so a ground there does not disturb the signal pairs, but tying pin 1 to DC ground at both ends between earthed devices is Rane's shields-grounded-both-ends loop; the unbalanced adapters must return on the pair-mate (2 or 6), which is what fixes the mini-TRS build. Field: Telos, Calrec (agent-reported) and Whirlwind all require shielded cable with pin 1 on the drain; field cables are more often unshielded, which is why the pin-4 option exists. Red team: two identical adapters in one drawer, one official and one PinRef, give "sometimes it hums, sometimes the condenser is dead" faults that follow whichever adapter was grabbed; the continuity beep between RJ45 pin 4 and XLR pin 1 is how you tell them apart.

Recommendation. For any cable that will plug into Axia, Telos or StudioHub boxes: follow the sheet, pin 1 to the shield, and buy shielded Cat5 with shielded plugs. For PinRef's "unshielded Cat5 works" variant: land pin 1 on pin 4 ONLY (StudioHub's own jumper position), leave pin 5 open, and label the cable as not for powered StudioHub runs or for Radial, Whirlwind or RAT snake boxes. PinRef's two-channel XLR3 and TRS builds currently put the second channel's ground on pin 5; the clean fix is to return channel 2's ground to pin 4 as well (two XLR pin 1s on one RJ45 pin) and leave pin 5 open. Confidence: high.
What changed in PinRef: 3.5 mm TRS to RJ45 re-pinned to the official 2/6 returns; the balanced XLR3 and TRS builds got explicit "PinRef variant, not interoperable with official StudioHub adapters for ground or phantom, never into a powered panel, PoE or other makers' snake boxes" notes. Moving channel 2's ground off pin 5 is proposed above and will be done if Jadon agrees (it is a wiring change to builds 6, 33, 27 and 35).
Decision (Jadon, 2026-11-09): apply the recommendation if it checks out. It does: the StudioHub+ Adapter Pinout Guide's universal pinout lists RJ45 pin 4 as DC GND and pin 5 as nc, and the SH-RACK4 manual p.15 (SH-2XLRMF panel, jumper JU2) reads "Install to connect XLR(s) pin 1 to StudioHub+ RJ45 power ground (RJ-45 pin 4)". Builds 6, 33, 27 and 35 now put BOTH channels' ground on RJ45 pin 4 and leave pin 5 open. Pins moved: channel 2's ground went from RJ45 pin 5 (White/Blue) to pin 4 (Blue); any two-channel cable built before needs channel 2's XLR pin 1 / TRS sleeve moved onto the Blue conductor at the breakout, at both ends of a run.

## Q4. RTS ADAM / KP keypanel RJ45 pairs versus the Clear-Com/ASG drawing.

Where it shows up. RTS ADAM and Cronus matrix frames driving KP-series keypanels over house Cat5, usually with a DB-9 at the keypanel and an RJ45 at a breakout; and the Adam IFB and camera keypanel adapters in trucks.

The conventions found. RTS's own RJ-45 breakout for the frame side, the XCP-48-RJ45: pin 2 keypanel data minus, pin 7 data plus, pins 3 and 6 audio out of the matrix, pins 4 and 5 audio into the matrix, 1 and 8 unused (RTS XCP-48-RJ45 manual, page 3). RTS's current keypanel jack, the KP-5032 matrix connector J8: RJ-45 pin 1 RS485+, 2 RS485−, 3 FROM MATRIX+, 4 TO MATRIX+, 5 TO MATRIX−, 6 FROM MATRIX−, 7 RS485+, 8 RS485−, footnoted "supports 568B and USOC wiring"; its RJ-12 column is 1 RS485−, 2 FROM MATRIX+, 3 TO MATRIX+, 4 TO MATRIX−, 5 FROM MATRIX−, 6 RS485+ (RTS KP-5032 Technical Manual, page 19). The KP-32 data sheet gives the same RJ-12 and the DB9S: 1 Data+, 2 Data−, 3 audio-in shield, 4 and 5 audio out to matrix, 6 data shield, 7 and 8 audio in from matrix, 9 audio-out shield (KP-32 Technical Data Sheet, page 2). The Clear-Com-hosted ASG drawing: data on the orange pair (1/2), listen from the frame on the blue pair (4/5), talk to the frame on the green pair (3/6) (Clear-Com Solution Finder, "RTS Adam DB9 to RJ45 Pin-Out"). PinRef's Cable Wholesale shells: data 1/2, from-matrix 3/6 (3 = +, 6 = −), to-matrix 4/5 with 5 = + and 4 = −.

Why RTS's RJ-45 looks odd. The XCP-48's data on 2 and 7 is not a 568B pair. It is the RJ-12 layout slid into the centre six positions of an 8-pin jack (RJ-12 pin n lands on RJ-45 pin n+1), so an RJ-12 keypanel plug physically inserted into the RJ-45 jack still works. The KP-5032 wires RS-485 to both 1/2 and 7/8 so that a 568B cable (data on 1/2) and a USOC cable (data on 2/7) both work. That is RTS acknowledging that installers wire these two ways.

How PinRef's shells compare, pin for pin. Against the KP-5032 J8 the Black shell matches on pins 1, 2, 3 and 6 exactly and differs only in the polarity of the TO MATRIX (talk) pair: RTS has 4 = + and 5 = −, the shell has 5 = + and 4 = −. Against the XCP-48-RJ45 the audio pairs match (with the same talk-polarity inversion) but data does not (the shell's data+ is on pin 1, the XCP's on pin 7). Against the ASG drawing the data pair matches and the two audio pairs are swapped. So the shells are closest to RTS's current keypanel jack, and the earlier PinRef note calling the pair plan "this rig's own" undersold them. The talk-polarity inversion is real and must stay in the notes until a shell is metered.

What happens when conventions are mixed on one run. Data: a 1/2 shell at one end and a 2/7 shell at the other leaves RS-485 minus connected and plus open, so the panel never comes up, or flickers up on short runs, which looks exactly like a flaky keypanel; the KP-5032's parallel wiring rescues this only when the panel end is a KP-4016/5032-class panel. Audio: an RTS-pattern shell at one end and an ASG shell at the other swaps talk and listen, so the frame's line output is wired into the panel's line output. Two balanced outputs driving each other is what Rane Note 109 warns about ("forcing both outputs into current-limit and possible damage"), and both inputs hear nothing. The deceptive case is PinRef at one end and ASG at the other: data matches, the panel comes up with names and lit keys, and there is silence both ways, so the tech starts changing settings on a working matrix. The earlier version of this file called mixing conventions "a troubleshooting problem, not a damage problem"; the output-to-output case means that was too soft. A straight TIA cable tester passes every one of these.

PinRef's Cronus RJ12-to-RJ45 adapter is a fourth layout (data on 3/6, matrix-out audio on 5/4, matrix-in audio on 1/2) and is incompatible with the Black and Yellow shells on a straight Cat5: patched together, the matrix's audio output meets the keypanel's talk output and the data lands on the listen input. Its use text also called it a "timing/tally adapter"; Cronus is a matrix intercom and the RJ-12 carries keypanel data and two audio pairs. Both corrected in the notes; the wiring is left as recorded because it may describe a physical adapter that mates with something else.

The five angles. Documentation: the DB-9 side is backed by the KP-32 sheet; the RJ-45 side has three published layouts and the shells match the KP-5032 except for talk polarity. Power: no DC on these lines; the hazard is a passive PoE injector (44 to 57 V across the audio transformers and the RS-485 transceiver) and the output-to-output fight when audio pairs are swapped. Circuit: all three layouts keep each balanced signal on a real pair except RTS's 2/7 data, which is a split pair that RTS gets away with because the partner conductors are unused. Field: RTS factory breakouts and anything with an RJ-12 use the RTS layout; patch-panel facilities use a 568B house layout and the house drawing decides which pair is talk; one convention per building, written on the shell. Red team: the costliest failure is the healthy-looking panel with no audio; the identifying test is continuity from DB9 pin 1 to RJ45 pin 1 (568B/ASG/PinRef) or pin 7 (RTS USOC), and from DB9 pin 4 to RJ45 pin 4 (RTS polarity) or pin 5 (PinRef).

Recommendation. Keep the shells as recorded, relabel them in the notes as "RTS keypanel RJ-45 (568B data) with the talk pair polarity inverted" rather than "this rig's own", and meter one known-good Black shell to confirm the talk polarity. Decide whether the inversion is deliberate (a house convention that is also on the matrix end) or an error; if it is an error on one shell only, swapping pins 4 and 5 at the RJ45 makes it a true RTS KP-5032 layout. For the Cronus adapter, record what it actually mates with; a compatible rewire is in its notes. Confidence: high on the conventions, medium on which one the physical shells follow until one is metered.
What changed in PinRef: KP-32 signal names added to every Adam shell wire; the KP-5032 comparison and talk-polarity note added; the Cronus adapter's use text corrected and flagged unverified; DE-9 gained an "RTS keypanel" pinout variant in the Pinouts tab so its face no longer shows RS-232 names for these builds.
Decision (Jadon, 2026-11-09): follow RTS. The Adam KP Black and Yellow shells and the Cronus adapter now use RTS's KP-5032 keypanel RJ-45 layout (1 RS485+, 2 RS485−, 3 FROM MATRIX+, 4 TO MATRIX+, 5 TO MATRIX−, 6 FROM MATRIX−; KP-5032 Technical Manual p.19), with the DE-9 per the KP-32 data sheet. Pins moved: on both shells DE-9 pin 4 went from RJ45 pin 5 to pin 4 and DE-9 pin 5 from RJ45 pin 4 to pin 5 (swap the two leads in DE-9 positions 4 and 5). On the Cronus adapter every RJ-12 pin moved: 1 from RJ45 3 to 2, 2 from 5 to 3, 3 from 1 to 4, 4 from 2 to 5, 5 from 4 to 6, 6 from 6 to 1. Data rides on RJ45 1/2, the only pair a DE-9 shell can carry; the KP-5032's own jack also repeats RS-485 on 7/8, but an RTS XCP-48-RJ45 breakout (data on 2/7 only) will not bring a panel up through these shells.

## Q5. Dynamic vs electret mics on intercom headsets.

Where it shows up. Every beltpack and keypanel headset jack. Mismatching the mic type is the most common "my headset is dead or distorted" call on a truck.

Which headsets use which. Clear-Com CC-300 and CC-400: dynamic, 200 ohm (Clear-Com CC-300 product page and Solution Finder). Clear-Com CC-15 and HS-15: electret, needing about 1.47 to 1.6 V DC (Clear-Com Solution Finder, CC-15/HS-15 bias). Telex PH-88 family: dynamic 200 ohm mic, dynamic 300 ohm receiver, in XLR-4F, XLR-4M and XLR-5M variants (Telex PH-88 Technical Data Sheet). Beyerdynamic DT 290 MK II: "Transducer type for microphone: Dynamic - Hypercardioid", supplied without cable, with K 190.28 (4-pin), K 190.40 (3-pin) and K 190.41 (5-pin) cable options (beyerdynamic DT 290 MK II product page). Beyerdynamic DT-108 and DT-109: dynamic, wired by Clear-Com straight to pins 1 and 2 (Clear-Com wiring note). Beyer DT-280 "v.11": a dynamic capsule with a built-in preamplifier that Tempest packs mis-detect as electret and then distort (Clear-Com Solution Finder, Tempest and DT-280). Sennheiser, from its own cable-compatibility sheet: HMD 27 is a dynamic hyper-cardioid and is listed with the X4F and X5 intercom cables; HME 27 is a pre-polarized condenser with the P48 amplifier integrated in the earcup, "P48 power supply is always necessary to power condenser microphone", and it is NOT listed with any intercom cable (Sennheiser "Broadcast Headsets HMS 27 overview cable compatibility", September 2021, page 2). Agent-reported: the Beyer K 190.41 5-pin cable is wired as a balanced mic with pin 1 hot and pin 2 low, the inverse of every intercom pinout, so a Beyer headset on an RTS pack needs the right cable, not just the right connector. Riedel elements were not reachable this pass and are not asserted.

How the packs bias and detect. Clear-Com RS-701: DIP switch 1, ON is Dynamic (default), OFF is Electret; the same jack carries about 15 V DC between pin 4 and pin 1 (RS-701 Quick Reference). Clear-Com's product table: RS-700 by DIP switch, V-Series and HelixNet by software with dynamic as default, FreeSpeak II and Edge and Tempest auto-detect, DX series electret only, Encore stations dynamic only (Clear-Com Solution Finder, "Which Clear-Com products support Electret headsets"). FreeSpeak II electret bias is 2.5 V DC, positive on pin 2 and negative on pin 1 through 1 k ohm (FSII-BP24 specification, agent-reported). Tempest decides dynamic at 600 ohm or less and electret at 1 k ohm or more (Clear-Com Solution Finder, Tempest pages). RTS BP-325: its MONO and STEREO headset jacks "are for monaural or stereo dynamic-mic headsets", with a separate carbon-mic jack; no electret mode and no bias (BP-325 manual, pages 7 and 13). RTS BTR-800 and TR-800 auto-detect and supply "a bias voltage, if needed" (BTR-800/TR-800 Operating Instructions, page 14, agent-reported). RTS KP-32 keypanels supply +5 V electret bias on the rear headset DB-9 pin 7 and on the panel-mic jack tip (KP-32 Technical Data Sheet, page 2).

What happens when it goes wrong. Electret headset on a pack set to dynamic: no bias, the mic is silent or hissy; the tech turns the gain up, gets bleed from the earpiece, and swaps the headset for another electret; on an Encore station or a BP-325 there is no electret setting at all, so the fix never comes. Dynamic headset on a pack set to electret: 1.5 to 2.5 V through 1 k ohm into a 200 ohm coil is about 2 mA of DC through the capsule, a thump on every key and a gain structure set for a hotter capsule; Clear-Com's manual says the wrong type "will degrade or nullify the audio ... or worse still, damage the microphone or headset" (V-Series online manual, "Headset connectors"). On an RS-701 the DIP switch is inside the pack, so a pack set to electret for one headset last tour silently biases every dynamic headset this tour. The Sennheiser trap: an HME 27 is a condenser, so a tech sets the pack to electret; the pack offers 1.5 to 2.5 V and the HME needs 48 V phantom, so the mic is dead and the switch gets blamed. Intercom headset mic into a mixer: a dynamic headset mic works as a low-output 200 ohm mic; an electret is dead because the mixer gives no bias. With 48 V phantom turned on into a headset-mic cable where pin 3 is tied to pin 1, the mixer pushes 48 V through one 6.8 k resistor into the capsule, about 7 mA DC: a dynamic coil pops and survives with an offset diaphragm, an electret built for 1.5 V can be destroyed; Yamaha's phantom article says never apply phantom to anything other than a condenser microphone (Yamaha, "Phantom Power"). The 5-pin trap: Telex KP98-7 keypanel headset jacks have pins 4 and 5 tied, so a keypanel-trained tech ties 4 and 5 in a 4-to-5-pin adapter for a beltpack; Clear-Com says that shorts two amplifier outputs and distorts the entire party line (Solution Finder, 4-pin to 5-pin adaptor); the fix is to leave pin 5 floating or set the BP-325's W5 jumper to mono.

What this did to PinRef's phone-headset builds. The TRRS-to-XLR4 and TRRS-to-XLR5 builds said "set the pack's mic switch to ELECTRET"; the BP-325 has no such switch, so on that pack those cables are listen-only, and the notes now say which packs bias an electret. Those same builds bond XLR pin 1 to pin 3 (a CTIA headset has one shared ground), which on a Clear-Com RS-700 pack puts the earbud across the 15 V between pins 4 and 1 and shorts the speaker return to the DC return; the notes now say not to use them on RS-700-series packs or any pack that shows DC between pins 4 and 1 or 1 and 3, and to meter first.

The five angles. Documentation: the manuals settle the pinouts, the bias values and, for Sennheiser, the P48 requirement; Riedel remains unverified. Power: bias is small (1.5 to 5 V), phantom is not, and the RS-700 15 V on pin 4 is the one headset-side rail that can hurt a capsule through a mis-wired adapter. Circuit: tie the mic's cold leg to pin 1 for a dynamic mic into a pack, which RTS and Telex themselves do; never let a shared-ground TRRS adapter bond pins 1 and 3 on a pack that has DC between them. Field: trucks standardise on dynamic headsets (CC-300, PH-88, DT-108, DT-290) so any pack works at default; electrets are the lightweight single-ear units and the DX wireless. Red team: the element type is invisible from the connector, two "electret" headsets can need completely different voltages (1.5 V bias versus 48 V phantom), and every mismatch looks like a dead headset.

Recommendation. Keep the two mic-into-beltpack builds dynamic-only as written, keep the "phantom off" lines on every headset-mic cable, state the element type and required bias on any headset-specific build, mark Sennheiser HME/HMDC as P48-only, and carry the "never short 4 and 5" warning on every 4-to-5-pin adapter. Confidence: high.
What changed in PinRef: the TRRS builds restrict talk to bias-supplying packs and carry the RS-700 DC warning; the XLR3-into-pack builds state genders per brand (Clear-Com packs have a male jack, the BP-325 a female receptacle); the party-line, phantom and 12 V XLR4 power mis-plug cautions were added across the intercom family.
Decision (Jadon, 2026-11-09): his headsets are Clear-Com and Sennheiser, very seldom RTS. From the makers' pages: Clear-Com CC-110, CC-220, CC-300 and CC-400 are dynamic (200 Ω); CC-15/CC-30 are electret (about 1.5 V); Sennheiser HMD 26/27 are dynamic (300 Ω); HME 26/27 are condensers on P48 and HMDC 27's noise compensation runs on P12–P48, and Sennheiser sells XLR-4F/XLR-5 intercom cables for the HMD only. Pack settings: RS-700 DIP 1 ON = Dynamic (default); HelixNet HBP-2X menu, 1.7 V in Electret; FreeSpeak II Auto (90–320 Ω = dynamic) or 2.5 V via 1 kΩ in Electret; V-Series software, about 1.5 V; party-line stations and the FreeSpeak II base are dynamic-only. The mic-into-pack builds (3, 9, 20) and headset-mic-to-mixer builds (21, 53) now say this; the phantom warnings stay. Nothing to re-pin.
